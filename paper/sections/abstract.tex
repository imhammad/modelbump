Providers of large language models (LLMs) retire model versions on set dates, and any application that still calls a retired model stops working. A recent study by \citet{kim2026retires} found that \KimAfterShutdown\% of open-source migrations away from a retired model happen only after its shutdown. We replicate and extend that study using a new dataset of \MigrationsAway{} commits from public GitHub repositories that move away from one of \ModelIds{} retired OpenAI, Anthropic and Google model IDs. We measured our migration label's precision on a blind, stratified sample (\Precision\%, 95\% CI \PrecisionLow--\PrecisionHigh). For models with exact retirement dates and at least 90 days of follow-up, \AfterLate\% (95\% CI \AfterLateLow--\AfterLateHigh) of migrations happen after the shutdown: the direction is the same as Kim's, but the share is lower than \KimAfterShutdown\%, and Kim's own labelling rules give \AfterKimLabel\% on our data. The median migration happens \ExactDays{} days after the shutdown, the busiest period is the first month after it, and projects that miss the deadline wait longer for OpenAI models than for Anthropic ones. Of the migrations that name a target, \FollowsLine\% follow the provider's recommended model line, yet \TargetOnList\% move to a model that is already scheduled for retirement. Only \TouchTest\% of sampled migration commits touch a test file, in line with Kim's \KimTouchTest\%, and \TouchEval\% touch an evaluation file. The results stay the same when commits are grouped by repository, when other labels are used, and when search gaps are weighted back in. We release the dataset and the code.
